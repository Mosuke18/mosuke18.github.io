About

I am a Ph.D. student at Keio University and a Research Assistant at the National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology (AIST), Japan.

My research focuses on music performance science, with particular interests in ensemble coordination, human movement during musical performance, and the perception and evaluation of ensemble performance.

I am especially interested in understanding how musicians coordinate with one another in large and small ensembles and how auditory, visual, and bodily information contribute to musical interaction and performance.

Alongside my research, I am actively involved in wind band performance and conducting, which motivates much of my scientific interest in ensemble performance.

Research

Ensemble Coordination

How do musicians coordinate their movements and musical actions with one another? I investigate interpersonal coordination in musical ensembles using motion capture and time-series analysis.

Perception and Evaluation of Musical Performance

I study how auditory and visual information influence judgments of musical performance. One line of research investigates the relative contribution of sound and visual information when listeners evaluate wind and brass ensemble performances.

Groove and Human Movement

I investigate relationships between musical rhythm, groove perception, and bodily movement, including how different motor contexts affect listeners' experiences of musical rhythm.

Music and Gait

I am also involved in research examining the use of sound- and music-based approaches for gait training and movement support.
